<section_guide number="2" title="Solution and Essential User Experiences" references="1">
<purpose>Propose one minimum Solution Strategy and the essential user experiences that work backward from the approved Desired Business Impact.</purpose>

<questions>
1. Ask only when a protected product decision cannot be safely proposed: which boundary should the PoC honor?
</questions>

<workflow>
Read Section 1 and keep its target user, core problem, and Desired Business Impact.

Work backward from the Desired Business Impact. Draft the smallest product-level Solution Strategy
and every Essential User Experience before asking the user to design either one. Present the proposal
for revision or approval.

Keep Section 2 to two inputs. Solution Strategy combines the product-level approach and minimum
user-visible PoC scope. Essential User Experiences list every experience the first PoC must not
omit. Each experience has a distinct name, a user-observable result, and a concise explanation of
how that result contributes to the Desired Business Impact.

Do not ask the user to supply a starting condition, sequential user actions, screen flow, or demo
procedure. Section 4 owns those execution choices. Ask one focused question only when multiple valid
choices would change money, permissions, law or regulation, privacy or safety, irreversible data
meaning, an external promise, or the acceptance boundary. Preserve every confirmed value, limit,
permission, and failure guarantee. Do not use implementation functions, internal state, technical
layers, or subjective impressions as completion results.
</workflow>

</section_guide>
